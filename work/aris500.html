Trading · 2026 · Research model with monthly trade lists

ARIS500

A systematic US equity model: five tested signal blocks, one composite score and a long-only portfolio built against the Russell 3000.

  • 13.27%annualised return net of costs, 2007 to 2025, against 10.45% for the Russell 3000
  • 0.75Sharpe ratio net of costs, against 0.62 for the index
  • 5signal blocks kept, out of 25 signals and blocks tested
  • 20×faster portfolio construction after moving the weight solver to C++

ARIS500 builds a point-in-time panel of US stocks from CRSP and Compustat, tests stock-selection signals one at a time, combines the five that pass into an equal-weighted score and holds the top tenth of the market under limits on position size, sector and beta. In a backtest of 227 months it returns 13.27% a year net of costs against 10.45% for the Russell 3000. Its own documentation says plainly that this does not yet prove alpha.

01What it is

ARIS500 is an equity model built the slow way round. It starts with the data: a monthly panel of every US common stock, where each accounting number is attached on the day it became public and not the day the quarter ended. Signals are tested on that panel one at a time against a fixed bar, and only then combined.

The output is not a trade. Each month the model writes a target portfolio and a trade list with a reason for every line, for a person to review. In its own words it is an overlay for investment judgment, not a claim that accounting data can replace fundamental work.

02Data that knows what day it is

Most backtest errors are timing errors: using a number before anyone could have seen it. The panel guards against that. Quarterly figures become available on their report date, annual figures six months after year end, and anything older than eighteen months is dropped. Delisted stocks stay in, with their final loss.

The investable set is one share class per company with a price of at least $5, a market value of at least $50 million and $500,000 of average daily trading. Because official index membership was not available, the universe is rebuilt each June from the 3,000 largest stocks; it tracks the real Russell 3000 to within 0.83% a year.

03Twenty-five ideas, five survivors

The bar for a signal is a t-statistic of 3 on its monthly rank correlation with next month's returns, together with a sensible long-short spread and an economic reason to exist. Low-risk and fundamental-momentum signals were tested and left out. Momentum is the one exception: it misses the bar on its own and was kept on the weight of prior evidence, which the signal log records.

  • Value. Cash-flow yield, earnings yield and EBITDA to enterprise value.
  • Quality. Returns on invested capital, return on equity and the stability of the operating margin.
  • Capital deployment. Net share reduction over 3, 12 and 36 months.
  • Momentum. The 12-month return, skipping the latest month.
  • Sentiment. Low short interest as a share of shares outstanding.

The composite gives each block 20%. No coefficient is fitted to realised returns.

04Inside the five blocks

A block is a small family of signals that measure the same thing in different ways. Averaging within a family reduces the noise in any one measure; keeping the families separate stops one idea from being counted twice.

Capital deployment has the strongest equal-weighted spread, quality the strongest among large companies. Value is the weakest once size is taken into account, which the documentation notes.

05Knowing what was known when

The panel has one row per stock per month and 379 columns. Most of the work is in the dates. A quarterly figure enters on the day it was reported; if that date is missing, three months after the quarter ended. Annual figures wait six months. Year-to-date cash flows are unpicked into single quarters so that a trailing-year sum is always four real quarters.

Before a panel is accepted it has to pass blocking checks: no duplicate rows, no accounting value used before its availability date, one investable share class per company, and a rebuilt benchmark that correlates at least 0.995 with the real index.

06Building the portfolio

The top tenth of stocks by score is held, weighted by each stock's weight in the benchmark so that the portfolio stays recognisably like the index. Positions too small to matter are dropped. A solver then enforces three limits at once: no stock above 3%, no sector more than ten points from its benchmark weight, and a portfolio beta between 0.95 and 1.10.

That solver took 97% of construction time, so it was rewritten in C++ and called from Python. The two versions agree bit for bit, which a test checks on 500 random problems and a 3,000-stock panel.

07The backtest

Over February 2007 to December 2025 the model returns 14.20% a year before costs and 13.27% after, against 10.45% for the Russell 3000. Volatility is slightly higher than the index's (16.74% against 15.97%), tracking error is 5.15%, and the information ratio net of costs is 0.52. The deepest loss, net, was 47.3%.

The repository stores no result files, so the path below is not the model's own series. It is the real index with a simulated active return laid over it, matched to those documented statistics.

08Running it each month

The monthly routine is deliberately manual at three points. The model pulls prices, rebuilds the panel and writes a dated target portfolio and trade list; a person then reviews any quarantined prices, approves the trades and reconciles the account afterwards.

  • Turnover: about 28% of the portfolio a month in the backtest, which is what the cost model charges for.
  • Active share: 82% on average, so the portfolio is far from an index copy even though it is weighted like one.
  • Concentration: the ten largest holdings average 29% of the portfolio.
  • Warnings on the trade list: any target with a report more than six months old, a price under $10 or a market value under $100 million is flagged for a second look.
  • Hard stops: the rebalance will not overwrite a dated output, and will not run from an empty current portfolio unless told it is the first one.

09What the result does not prove

The documentation is direct about the limits, and so is this page.

  • The alpha is not significant. After the standard factors are taken out, 2.05% a year remains, with a t-statistic of 1.91. The project's own bar is 3.
  • It is concentrated in time. Six of the 227 months produced half of the cumulative excess return.
  • Some of it is factor exposure. Tilts explain 22.9% of the attributed active return, and momentum is the one significant loading.
  • The beta limit has a material effect. It binds in two months out of three.
  • The data has edges. Accounts can be restated, the universe is a proxy, and prices after December 2025 come from a second source and are provisional.
