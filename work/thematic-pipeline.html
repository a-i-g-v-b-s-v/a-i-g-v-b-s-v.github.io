Thematic Pipeline

A pipeline that turns an investment theme into a weighted list of stocks, by scoring companies on their accounts and by having a language model read their annual reports.

  • 24subthemes across 4 themes
  • 4language models compared for reading filings
  • 117stocks in the final high-conviction portfolio
  • 0.81Sharpe ratio in backtest, against 0.82 for equal weighting the same stocks

Plintus Capital Management invests by theme: defence, healthcare, the energy transition, critical minerals. The question the pipeline answers is which companies belong in each theme and how much of each to hold. It starts from the holdings of thematic funds, scores every company on momentum, quality, growth and valuation, and then has a language model read each company's annual report to measure how much of the business the theme really is. The two scores together set the weight.

01The problem

A theme is easy to name and hard to buy. A fund labelled for a theme can hold hundreds of companies, and some of them have little to do with it. Two questions follow: which of these companies are good businesses at a fair price, and which of them are really exposed to the theme?

The first is a factor question and can be answered from accounts and prices. The second is a reading question, and until recently it needed an analyst for every company.

02Scoring the business

Every company is ranked within its subtheme on four groups of factors, and the ranks are combined into one score. The weights change where the standard set does not fit: early-stage biotech has no earnings to judge quality by, so quality is dropped, and for traditional energy the dividend replaces valuation.

Companies are matched across databases by security identifier and never by ticker, because tickers are reused.

The factors
GroupMeasures
MomentumReturn over months 12 to 2 before the date, skipping the latest month
QualityGross profit to assets; return on equity
GrowthRevenue growth over the prior year
ValuationFree cash flow, earnings and sales, each against market value
HygieneAccounts lagged 90 days so nothing is used before it was published; extremes clipped at the 1st and 99th percentiles

03Reading the filings

Each company's annual report describes its business in its own words. The pipeline splits that text into passages, has a model compare each passage with a description of the subtheme, and keeps the strongest matches as the company's thematic score.

The model mattered more than I expected. Defence companies write about their work in careful, indirect language, and simpler methods scored them near zero. The method that worked best asks a different question: given this filing, is a plain statement about the theme true?

Four ways of reading a filing
ModelHow it scoresWhat happened
Word counts (TF-IDF)Shared vocabulary between the theme and the filingNear zero for defence, where filings avoid plain words.
MiniLM sentence embeddingsSimilarity of meaning, small modelScores of 0.05 to 0.10: too weak to separate companies.
BGE-large embeddingsSimilarity of meaning, with an instruction describing the searchScores of 0.40 to 0.80 for good matches; 352 stocks weighted.
DeBERTa-v3, entailmentAsks whether the filing supports a written hypothesis about the themeThe sharpest separation; a 117-stock high-conviction portfolio.
e5-mistral embeddingsA larger embedding model, run overnight283 stocks weighted; no clear gain over BGE for the cost.

04What the backtest showed

The final portfolio was tested over ten years against the S&P 500 and against the same stocks held in equal weights. It beat the index comfortably. It did not beat equal weighting.

Two things should be said plainly. First, the text weighting added nothing measurable: the selection did the work and the weights did not. Second, the list of stocks was built from today's fund holdings and today's filings, then tested on the past ten years. Companies that are in thematic funds now are, in part, companies that did well. That flatters the comparison with the index, and a proper test would rebuild the list at each date.
