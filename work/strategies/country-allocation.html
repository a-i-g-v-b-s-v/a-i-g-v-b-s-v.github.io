Country allocation

Nineteen stock markets, tilted toward the ones with the strongest momentum.

A monthly, long-only rotation between single-country equity funds. The design has four signals; the recorded runs used only momentum, because the valuation and yield data were never connected.

01The idea

Country returns differ far more than most global portfolios allow for, and the differences persist for months. A portfolio that leans toward the leaders should beat one that holds every market equally.

02The signal

The design weights are below. In every recorded run only the first was active.

  • Momentum, 40%. Twelve-month return, skipping the latest month.
  • Value, 30%. Cyclically adjusted price-earnings ratio. Not connected.
  • Carry, 15%. Dividend yield. Not connected.
  • Growth surprise, 15%. Not built.

03Sizing and risk

Equal-weight base, each country tilted by half its score, 15% cap, long only. Rebalanced monthly. None.

04Results on record

Two periods are on record: a Sharpe ratio of 0.46 from 2010 and 0.66 from 2015, with a 7.5% annual return and a 26.7% drawdown. No return series was saved for this strategy, so the figures are quoted from the run records.

05What works, and what does not

  • Works: the best of the fund-based modules that has a clean record.
  • Mostly beta: a long-only tilt around equal weight behaves much like a world equity fund, which the 27% drawdown shows.
  • Three of four signals are missing, and the tilt is small, so the portfolio barely leaves equal weight.
