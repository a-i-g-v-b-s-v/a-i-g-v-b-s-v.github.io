Commodity momentum and value

The coursework study behind A.R.I.S: 24 commodities ranked on momentum and value, monthly, over 24 years.

  • 1.83Sharpe ratio over the full sample, 2002 to 2025, as reported in the notebook

A long-short strategy on the 24 constituents of the S&P GSCI, built as the final project for a quantitative portfolio management course. Its recommended form uses two signals, momentum and value, and is the source of the 1.83 Sharpe ratio quoted for the commodity signal.

01The idea

Commodities that have risen tend to keep rising for some months, and commodities far above their long-run price tend to fall back over some years. The two effects work on different horizons, so they can be held together.

02The signal

The recommended two-signal form, with equal weight:

  • Momentum, 50%. Average of standardised returns over 3, 6, 9 and 12 months.
  • Value, 50%. Distance from the 24- to 60-month average price, with momentum's influence removed.

03Sizing and risk

Sign of the score, equal weight; a fuller version targets 15% volatility with a 2.5× leverage cap. Rebalanced monthly. None in the baseline.

04Results on record

The notebook reports a 15.2% annual return at 8.3% volatility for the two-signal baseline, a Sharpe ratio of 1.83, against 0.11 for the index itself. Split at December 2012, the first half gives 1.21 and the second, unseen half 1.31.

The bars show how the figure moves as the test gets harder. No return series was saved for this strategy, so the figures are quoted from the run records.

05What works, and what does not

  • Reported: a Sharpe ratio of 1.83 over the full sample, 1.31 out of sample when split at the end of 2012, and a bootstrap interval of 1.37 to 2.29.
  • The notebook's own caveats: parameters were chosen with knowledge of the full sample, and after a haircut for that the estimate falls to between 0.65 and 1.28.
  • Unresolved: one diagnostic table in the notebook shows the signals' edge disappearing when they are lagged a further month. That has to be explained before the headline number can be relied on.
  • An earlier version on free data had a Sharpe ratio of −0.82. The gap between the two is the data.
