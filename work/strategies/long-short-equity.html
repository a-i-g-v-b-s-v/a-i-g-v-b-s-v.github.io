The largest module in the book. Each stock gets a composite score from four families of signal built on CRSP, Compustat and IBES data. The portfolio is fully long the top quintile and 30% short the bottom one.

01The idea

No single stock signal works all the time. Momentum does well in trends and badly at turns, value the opposite, and analyst revisions carry information that prices absorb slowly. Combining them gives a steadier ranking than any one alone.

02The signal

Four components, each a cross-sectional z-score clipped at three standard deviations:

  • Momentum, 30%. Twelve-month return, excluding the latest month.
  • Quality and value, 30%. Return on invested capital, free-cash-flow yield, Piotroski score and low leverage, lagged six months.
  • Earnings revisions, 30%. Change in the analyst consensus, breadth of revisions and earnings surprise.
  • Short interest, 10%. Low short interest scores high.

03Sizing and risk

15% volatility target, at most 2× leverage; 7% per name; net sector exposure capped at 25%. Rebalanced monthly. Trading halts at a 15% drawdown and resumes at 7.5%.

04Results on record

The run of record, from March 2026, covers 2005 to 2024: a Sharpe ratio of 1.25, a 16.0% annual return at 12.7% volatility and a 14.9% drawdown. Two later runs over shorter periods give 1.55 and 1.85.

These should be read with the timing flaw under limits in mind. No return series was saved for this strategy, so the figures are quoted from the run records.

05What works, and what does not

  • Works: signal construction, portfolio rules and a paper-trading connector that placed orders in March 2026.
  • Known flaw in the backtest: each month's weights are matched with that same month's returns. A one-month lag is noted in the code but not applied, so the recorded Sharpe ratios are overstated and the test needs re-running.
  • Conflicting records: three runs give three different answers, and the validation report annualised a monthly series as if it were daily.
