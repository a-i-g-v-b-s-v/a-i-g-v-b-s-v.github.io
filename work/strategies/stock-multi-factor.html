Eight-factor long-short stocks

01The idea

Stock factors are well documented, so the first attempt combined eight of them. The catch is that the best-documented factors need accounting data. Rebuilt from prices alone, value becomes distance from a five-year average and quality becomes return over volatility, and neither proxy is the real thing.

02The signal

Eight signals, each a rank across the universe:

  • Cross-sectional momentum, 20%. Twelve-month return against other stocks.
  • Time-series momentum, 15%. Each stock against its own past.
  • Value proxy, 15%. Distance below the five-year average price.
  • Quality proxy, 15%. One-year return divided by volatility.
  • Trend, 15%. Moving-average crossovers.
  • Low volatility, 10%. Calmer stocks.
  • Reversal, 5%. The last month, reversed.
  • Volatility mean reversion, 5%. Stocks whose volatility is unusually high.

03Sizing and risk

Inverse-volatility weights, 12% volatility target, 5% position cap, 1.5× leverage cap. Rebalanced every 21 trading days. Linear de-leveraging once drawdown passes 15%.

04Results on record

The backtest file on disk runs from 2016-01 to 2026-03. Over that period the strategy returns -1.1% a year at 9.2% volatility, a Sharpe ratio of -0.12 with no risk-free rate deducted, and its deepest month-end drawdown is 22.4%. Costs of 5 basis points per trade are included.

05What works, and what does not

  • Failed: a negative return over ten years, with a drawdown above 20%.
  • Why: price-only proxies for value and quality are mostly noise, and shorting large US stocks through 2016–2021 was expensive.
  • What it led to: trading asset classes instead of single stocks, and adding a market-regime test.
