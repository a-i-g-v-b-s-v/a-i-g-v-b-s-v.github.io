Dual momentum with a regime switch

Hold the strongest funds while the stock market is in an uptrend, and move to bonds when it is not.

A monthly rotation across 21 exchange-traded funds. Each fund is scored on momentum, trend and stability; a single market-trend test decides whether the portfolio is mostly in risk assets or mostly in bonds.

01The idea

Two questions, asked once a month. Which assets have been strongest? And is this a market worth being in at all? The first picks the holdings, the second sets how much of the portfolio they get. It is the second version of this system, built after a stock-level version failed.

02The signal

Each fund's score has three parts. A fund with a negative twelve-month return has its score cut to 30%.

  • Momentum, 50%. Returns over 1, 3, 6 and 12 months, weighted 10/20/30/40.
  • Trend, 25%. Whether price is above its 50-, 100- and 200-day averages.
  • Stability, 25%. Inverse of recent volatility.

03Sizing and risk

Inverse-volatility weights, 10% annual volatility target, no leverage. Rebalanced monthly. S&P 500 above its 200-day average: 80% in the top third of risk funds, 20% in bonds. Below it: 20% in the top three, 80% in bonds.

04Results on record

The backtest file on disk runs from 2015-01 to 2026-03. Over that period the strategy returns 6.7% a year at 9.0% volatility, a Sharpe ratio of 0.75 with no risk-free rate deducted, and its deepest month-end drawdown is 14.7%. Costs of 5 basis points per trade are included.

05What works, and what does not

  • Works: the best risk-adjusted result of the four versions, with drawdowns about half those of the stock market over the same years.
  • Limit: a 200-day rule is slow. It leaves late and returns late, and loses in sideways markets.
  • Not tested: out of sample. The rules were chosen on the same years they are scored on.
