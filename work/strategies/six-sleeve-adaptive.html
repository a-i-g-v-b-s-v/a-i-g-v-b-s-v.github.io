Six sleeves, adaptively weighted

Six small strategies run side by side, with more capital going to whichever has been working.

  • 0.75Sharpe ratio, 2015 to 2026, from the saved backtest

  • 6sleeves blended by recent performance

Momentum, carry, trend, mean reversion, volatility and quality sleeves over 20 funds. Each sleeve proposes weights; the portfolio blends them by their performance over the last six months and scales the total by a four-part reading of market health.

01The idea

Every style has seasons. Instead of choosing one, this version runs six and lets recent results decide the mix, on the view that a style which has stopped working usually stays that way for a while.

02The signal

Six sleeves, equal to begin with, then re-weighted by rolling 126-day performance. Shown here at their starting weights:

  • Momentum, 16.7%. Strongest funds over the past year.
  • Trend, 16.7%. Funds above their moving averages.
  • Carry, 16.7%. Higher-yielding funds.
  • Mean reversion, 16.7%. Funds that have fallen unusually far.
  • Volatility, 16.7%. Calmer funds.
  • Quality, 16.7%. Steadier return per unit of risk.

03Sizing and risk

12% annual volatility target, scaling capped at 1.5×. Rebalanced monthly. Regime score from 0 to 1: S&P 500 against its 50- and 200-day averages, high-yield against investment-grade credit, recent volatility, and breadth.

04Results on record

The backtest file on disk runs from 2015-01 to 2026-03. Over that period the strategy returns 6.2% a year at 8.2% volatility, a Sharpe ratio of 0.75 with no risk-free rate deducted, and its deepest month-end drawdown is 13.4%. Costs of 5 basis points per trade are included.

05What works, and what does not

  • Works: the smoothest of the four versions.
  • Limit: adaptive weighting chases its own tail when leadership rotates quickly.
  • Cost of complexity: six sleeves and a regime score give many parameters for one decade of data.
