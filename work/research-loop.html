Research Loop

A pipeline that turns a strategy idea into a tested rule and then tries to reject it. Most ideas fail, which is what it is for.

  • 4checks a strategy must pass at once
  • 6strategy families it can test, from momentum to pairs
  • 4 of 4runs on this page rejected, including two on real markets
  • 11unit tests, all passing

Most backtests are flattering because the person running them keeps trying until one works. The Research Loop removes that freedom. An idea goes in as a sentence or a paper, a language model turns it into a precise rule, and the rule is backtested and put through four checks it must pass together. Every attempt is counted, so trying twenty variants makes the twenty-first harder to pass. Only a rule that survives is allowed near a broker.

01Why it exists

After building twenty-two strategies I had learned the uncomfortable part: a backtest tells you as much about how many things you tried as about the idea. One of mine showed a Sharpe ratio of 2.6 that fell to about zero when a look-ahead bug was fixed.

So I wrote the discipline down as code. The loop takes an idea, writes it as an exact rule, tests it, and gives a verdict with the reason. It keeps a ledger of every trial, and the more trials there are, the higher the bar.

02The four checks

A strategy survives only if it passes all four. Each one targets a different way a backtest lies: fitting the past, fading out of sample, being one lucky draw among many, and profiting from the path of prices instead of from any pattern in them.

The placebo is the one I trust most. The same rule is run on a hundred versions of the price series with the returns shuffled. If it makes money on those too, it was never reading a pattern.

The four checks (all must pass)
CheckWhat it asksThreshold
Out-of-sample SharpeDoes it still work on the half of the data it was not fitted on?at least 0.5
DecayHow much of the fitted result survives out of sample?at least half
Deflated SharpeIs the result still significant after counting every variant I tried?probability of 0.80
PlaceboDoes it beat the same rule run on shuffled prices?better than 90 of 100

03What happened when I ran it

I ran the loop four times for this page, on 15 November 2026. It rejected all four. The first is the control and should fail. The second shows that even a series built to trend does not pass easily. The last two are real markets.

Bitcoin momentum is the instructive one. It passes three checks with a tested Sharpe ratio of 0.71, and by most standards would be called a result. It is rejected because it beats only 88 of 100 shuffled paths.

04Research done with it

The loop also runs on monthly data for US stocks from CRSP, restricted to the largest thousand companies priced above five dollars. I used it to re-test momentum with the rule frozen before looking at the data.

What it has found on real stock data
QuestionFinding
Does stock momentum hold in its original sample, 1963 to 1989?Yes: a Sharpe ratio of about 1.05 on the frozen rule.
Does scaling by volatility improve it?Over the whole period, from 0.54 to 0.68. After 2010, no gain.
Do a single split and a walk-forward agree?No. They disagree, which is itself a warning about either one alone.
Does pairs trading by price distance work?Rejected on 23 exchange-traded funds and on 62 large companies.
