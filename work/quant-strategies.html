01One frame, many strategies

The strategies differ in what they trade and why, but most are built the same way. Each turns its own inputs into a composite score, scales positions to a volatility target, applies position and leverage limits and produces one daily return series, with weights lagged a day so that nothing trades on information it could not have had.

That sameness makes a currency carry trade and an equity pairs trade comparable line by line. It also makes failures legible: when a module does not work, the frame is not what is wrong.

02What the record shows

The chart ranks every strategy that has a result on record. It is not a flattering picture, and it is the accurate one. The fund-based systems with saved return series sit between 0.6 and 0.75. The long-short equity module records the highest figures, but its backtest has a timing flaw that overstates them. Most of the remaining modules are close to zero, and five are negative.

The 5 strategies with no stored result are left off the chart. Periods and methods differ between bars, so the comparison is rough.

03Where the ideas come from

Signal ideas start in the literature. A scraper runs 61 search queries over arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, SSRN, NBER and the research pages of systematic managers, removes duplicates and classifies each paper into one of nine strategy families by keyword. It has collected 1,049 papers, held in a local database with a small search interface.

The classifier needs no model and no API key. Its weakness is false positives: papers from outside finance that share the vocabulary.

04Combining them

An orchestrator runs the book in eight steps: refresh data, read the regime, compute signals, build the portfolio, check risk, execute, reconcile and report. Each module's weight is proportional to its Sharpe ratio, floored at zero, with a minimum per regime. The macro overlay then scales gross exposure, from full in an expansion to half in a deflationary crisis, and circuit breakers cut it further when the VIX or the day's loss crosses a threshold.

One combined result is on record. Weighting the modules with positive Sharpe ratios gives 7.3% a year at 8.0% volatility from 2010 to March 2026, a Sharpe ratio of 0.91 against 0.64 for the S&P 500. It should be read loosely: the weights were set with knowledge of the full period, and most of the weight sits in the long-short equity module.

05What works today, and what does not

  • Works: the shared frame, the scraper, the validation suite, the regime overlay and a paper-trading connection to Interactive Brokers that ran for two and a half weeks in March 2026.
  • Honest scorecard: three fund-based systems with Sharpe ratios of 0.6 to 0.75 on saved data; one coursework commodity strategy with a strong but caveated result; and a book of modules most of which do not yet earn their place.
  • Common causes: free data standing in for the real thing (fund prices for futures, index changes for tradable volatility), data feeds that fail silently, and modules so lightly invested that their results describe almost nothing.
  • To fix first: the signal timing in the long-short equity backtest, and the allocation rule, which uses each module's full-period Sharpe ratio.
